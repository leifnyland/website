Low Speed Ramjet v1

Although more of a torch and less of a motor due to the low speed design, this is my first attempt to make a homemade ramjet following the designs by Larry Cottrill for his Maggie Muggs Low Speed Ramjet.

Objective

PLEASE, PLEASE BE CAREFUL!!

If you decide, of your own free will, to try an recreate anything I do here, you will HURT YOURSELF. Flamable fuels are far more flamable than you think, and you will get hurt. Please be safe.

I found these plans after I became aware of the Boom Prize and began thinking about various powerplant solutions. Although it quickly became clear that this motor has no direct feasability, it is a cool learning opportunity with very few well documented success stories online (maybe worth noting for safety reasons).

I have a few goals with this project:

  • Expand my hands on experience with non-reciprocating engines
  • Learn about similar simple thrust engines, eg pulese jets, afterburners, and turbo/ram jets
  • Give me an exuse to read some aero textbooks and get some experience on fluid flow/drag modeling software

Steps

The Build

I set the goal to use as many parts as I could from around my home and from thrift stores. There's something I love about making things from junk (its cheap).

Junk List:

  • stainless steel insulated coffee cup from goodwill
  • stainless steel childrens sippy ladybug bottle from local thrift
  • 10mm bolt, nut, and 2 conduit straps from "the bin"
  • needle valve from model airplane engine I had since I was 10 (O.S. MAX 35FP?)
  • rubbing alcohol bottle
  • screen gasket for injector gasket
  • trusty 2x8 scrap
Store parts list:
  • JB Weld
  • sink drain strainer
  • 5/32 brass tubing
  • 3/16 vinyl tubing
  • replacement valve stem
  • rechargable, windproof lighter

Here is an image of some mugs cut up so that I had varios sizes and shapes of single walled mugs. I then picked 2 mugs out of the pile with no real expertise, and instead just used a lot of what felt about mixed with a lot of trying to match sizes.

These photos show various angles of the final major metal mug parts before assembly. Note the similar diameters of all 3 parts.

This is the first dryfit. The exit nozzle needed to be stretched to fit over the rest of the motor.

Here is the injector and throttle used for all operation of this engine.

The injector is made out of a small length of 5/32 brass tubing. The bottom end is plugged with JB Weld, and then a small hole was punched on either side as a fuel nozzle. The brass tube is held in place with a custom gasket made from housing screen gasket material, which works well due to it's tubelike shape but struggles under direct flame exposure. The friciton fit allows for the whole assembly to be easily removed, but the gasket has to be replaced after every 1-5 minutes of opperation.

The throttle is made from a needle valve which was salvaged from a glow fuel model RC engine. The output was expanded to 1/16 internal diameter by drilling it out to increase fuel flow rate.

After several test runs, I believe this has significant room for improvement. This injector does not work well at supplying fuel purely based on the pressure of the surrounding airflow, and therefore requires a preasurized fuel line.

Simple fuel tank with a schrader stem to use as a "fuel pump." This tank works best when fuelled to 1/3 full or less, as the fuel pressure stays more consistent. The container has now begun to wear out under the pressure of opperation.

Here is the motor after the first test runs. Note the nice color change on the rear nozzle showing overall good heat distribution and minimal thermal load on the JB Weld bonds.

Trial Run 1

These videos show the first successes of the maggie mugs ramjet. There is quite a trick to controlling air, fuel, and spark with only 2 hands, but I started to get the hang of the starup conditions and also how to make the motor run. The needle valve had not been correctly adjusted after porting it out, so the throttle could never be turned completely off which led to some rather apparent safty concerns. This was fixed for future runs.

Trial Run 2

This is the first run where opperation was stable enough to begin playing with the throttle and begin testing the build dimentions of the motor. The use of a larger shop vac created more stable airflow and better cooling. The fuel line began to suck in air before I felt complete. In a later run (not reccorded), I was able to fully attempt to lean out the mixture and pull the flame back into the combustion chamber, with minimal success. I believe the dimentions of this motor are wrong, and it could benifit from a longer combustion chamber to utilize more of the thermal expansion from the fuel.

Lessons Learned/Next Steps

This motor was built to teach me some basics of small scale, airbreathing thrust motors. Although this motor probaly never produced positive thrust, it built a good fountation for liquid fuel delivery, slapjob construction under high thermal loads, and also built some good safety practices.

My next steps are to begin looking into inline pulsejets of a similar scale, as their construction is the most attainable with my current tools and I would like to have the chance of producing positive thrust with my next motor.
